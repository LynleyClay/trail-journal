#  I asked for a lifeline and got a power line


I was in the highest part of Colorado. I had just passed over Grays and Torreys and taken the alternate to Argentine Spine. The trail was unmarked, I was by myself, and a snowstorm was coming the next day. I had such a good day enjoying the beautiful views, and then I went to plug my almost dead phone into my battery pack, only to realize it hadn't been charging while I was in town. Rookie mistake. Learn from me: always make sure your battery pack is actually charging when you plug it in.

My heart sank. I was on an alternate, with no trail, no signs, by myself, with no hikers within 20 miles of me, and no way to tell where I was going. To make things worse, the cold was draining my phone battery even faster. I had a mild panic attack and went to bed.

The next morning I tried to memorize my map on my phone, put it away, and started hiking. Within a mile the trail forked and I had no idea which way was correct. I didn't have enough food to get lost, and I could be lost for a long time in that area. I desperately pulled out my map again. 6% left, with two more days to town.

I saw something on the map. Power lines that led to a tiny town with a population of 40: Montezuma, Colorado. That was my chance. I got to the power line, butt scooted down the side of a very steep mountain, and then bushwhacked my way through the woods until I hit the road. I still wasn't sure which way to go once I was on it, and I was a little nervous, given I was in the middle of nowhere on a gravel road, fully off my route at this point.

A little further along I saw a piece of paper sitting on the road. I picked it up, and what do you know, it was a map with written directions and a highlighted route to Montezuma. I started shaking and dropped to my knees. I was saved. I laughed. I think that makes me the luckiest person in the world. My friends always joke that I get the best trail magic, and I tell them it's because I do something dumb and then desperately need it. I made it to the town and there was a kid standing there in the middle of the road, no older than seven or eight.

"Are you a hiker?" he asked. "I am," I replied. "You can stay at my house." Well, not sure how his parents would feel about that, but that was easy enough. I followed him to his house and met his mom. She was a very nice teacher, and she welcomed me in and let me charge my phone.

It happened to be that the day I arrived, the local fire department had come to teach the locals how to fight their own fires. The town was too remote to wait on the fire department if there was an actual fire, so they had a local setup that would hold it off long enough. We had sandwiches and trained on the water hose. It was quite a unique experience.

The best part about Montezuma is they believe in free range dogs. Everyone's dogs roamed around, and the locals were some of the nicest people I've ever met, a true community. That evening, the family that took me in drew out a map for me, an alternate I could take to get to my next stop: Breckenridge. It was a beautiful trail. I got to hike over different ridges and through a ghost town, and they let me stay in a cabin they maintained along the way.

I learned a lot on that stretch, and I was saved from my own irresponsibility, but I also learned that sometimes our biggest mistakes become our best memories.
